# Facade and multi-face workflow

`RUNTIME_ROOT` is the user's verified runtime folder (runtime.md). The helper commands below need its light Python (`node scripts/setup.mjs --tools`, once); without it, read the photo directly and say which check was skipped.

The optional detector is usable only with separately available weights licensed for the user's intended purpose. Do not download the restricted upstream studio weights. Skip detector guidance if that prerequisite is not met.

Scope rule: model the building(s) the user requested. Keep observed, assumed and occluded/inferred details distinct. Opening boxes include the opening's own frame; decorative surrounds are generated around them. Older upstream examples and dataset measurements are not accuracy guarantees for this adaptation.

## Reading procedure

1. **Which part.** One face seen straight: a `facade`. The whole building, or two or more faces: one `block` with a face per visible side. A curved or free-form volume: photo3d.md.
2. **Already straightened photos** often have grey or black wedges outside the facade: they are not part of the building. Set `px` to the facade itself; where a regular column of windows runs into a wedge, continue the rhythm only as far as the facade's real edge and say it is inferred.
3. **Photo taken at an angle** (facade a trapezoid, verticals converging): read only the four facade corners, then straighten it and read the result instead:
   `node "RUNTIME_ROOT/scripts/run-tool.mjs" rectify PHOTO "x,y x,y x,y x,y" OUT.jpg [--ratio W/H]` with corners top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left (if `run-tool` finds no Python, tell the user to run `node scripts/setup.mjs --tools` in `RUNTIME_ROOT`). Pass `--ratio` when the real proportions are known (bay count x bay width over floor count x floor height); otherwise they are estimated from the corners (a few % off). Read OUT with `px:[0,0,OUT width,OUT height]`. 
4. **Facade photo with `detect_facade` listed** (a local detector trained on facade datasets): call it on the image you will read (the straightened one from step 3, or the photo itself), then correct its draft instead of reading every element. Keep its windows, sills, cornices, mouldings, balconies, blinds and deco; check doors, shops and pillars yourself (its weak classes); start from its proposed `px` and move it to the facade's own corners and `h`; merge overlapping duplicates (stacked mouldings, pillar fragments into one shaft), drop boxes on neighbouring buildings and tiny false shops, but keep dormers and pediment ornaments of this building; add `rustic`/`quoin`. Treat the detector result as a draft and apply the reading rules below.
5. **Inventory before the spec, check after the build.** The model must hold the same elements as the photo; a missing pediment makes the reading useless whatever the window accuracy. Before writing the spec, list in one compact line per group what the photo shows: volumes (wings, risalit, setbacks, lower bodies), roof (type, dormers and where, chimneys, parapet, finials), crown (main cornice, attic, pediments, gables), vertical order (lesenes, pilasters, columns, quoins), each floor (opening count, shape rect/arch/seg/round, hoods tri/seg/flat, sills, panels, balconies and railing type), ground floor (portico or arcade, shops and signs, doors, plinth, basement vents, steps), materials and colours. Map every item to a spec element; when none fits, say so in one line rather than dropping it silently. Never add what the photo does not show (a roof hidden behind a cornice stays flat, no invented dormers, and the reply says the roof is assumed). After building, compare the model with the photo item by item (`notes` in the result lists elements that fell outside the facade) and fix what is missing.
   - A triangular or segmental hood over one window is a `cornice` with `shape:"tri"` or `"seg"` (`"flat"` otherwise); `k:"pediment"` is for the gable crowning a risalit or the whole front.
   - A pediment over a risalit is `k:"pediment"` with its full box, rising above the eaves; an oculus or a relief inside it is kept. A cornice-height `molding` is not a pediment.
   - `palette` takes the photo's colours (wall, trim, frames, glass, shutters, doors, railings, roof); `detect_facade` proposes one from the image.
   - A risalit or bay that stands forward is a second facade part over its width with `at` moved a few tens of cm towards the street.
   - An open portico, loggia or pilotis floor is `k:"arcade"` (shape arch/rect/seg, `d` = how deep the recess is): one box per arch; doors and windows read inside an arch are built on its back wall. Do not read an open arch as a shop window.
   - Oculi and round windows: `shape:"round"`. Round piers and free or engaged columns on a facade: `k:"pillar", shape:"round"` (`d` = how far it stands out; the diameter for a free column); a classical order spanning floors can also be a `column` part placed in front.
   - Railings: `rail:"iron"` for wrought iron, `"glass"` for glass or frosted panels between posts, `"solid"` for a masonry or concrete parapet, `"balustrade"` for stone balusters.
   - Modern buildings: `style:"modern"` (slim dark profiles, glass doors), `k:"spandrel"` for the dark panels between ribbon windows, plain `molding` for slab edges and eaves.
   - The wall's outline above the eaves is part of the reading: a gable end to the street, a stepped or shaped gable, a parapet raised in the middle (false front) go in `top`; set `px`/`h` at the eaves (the side corners) and keep the gable's windows, mouldings and pilasters.
   - Materials by zone: a stone base, a timber ground floor or a brick wing on a rendered wall is `clad` over that zone (or a second facade part with its own `palette` when it is a separate volume); a different colour on one element is its `color`.
   - Doors: glazed and patio doors `leaf:"glass"` (with `bars`), garage doors `leaf:"garage"`. Downpipes are `pipe`s.
   - Dormers: keep the dormer windows read above the eaves on a facade with `depth` and a pitched roof: they become dormers at those positions. Chimneys: `chimneys:[x, ...]` in photo pixels where the stacks stand.
6. **Image to 3D**: read the image once and don't describe it back. For a frontal elevation use `facade` with `px`: read pixel corners straight off the photo, one element per box, `grid` for every regular row or column of identical elements (it is the biggest token saver). Set `h` from floor count (about 3 m per modern floor, 4-4.5 m per historic one) or a known size (door 2.1-2.4 m). Read the outline first (`px` = this building only: stop at neighbours, sky and pavement), then openings row by row, then the mouldings, sills and hoods that frame them. Reading rules:
   - One box per separately framed sash: a triple window with solid piers between the panes is three windows, not one wide one.
   - Tall or uneven facades: give the real start of every column and row with `xs:[...]` / `ys:[...]` (pixels, same as `box`) instead of one `grid` step. A uniform step can accumulate alignment errors on tall facades.
   - Facades are regular: if a column of windows repeats on every floor, a pane hidden by a tree, shadow or sign may continue the rhythm; label it as inferred, and do not represent it as directly observed.
   - Read thin elements at their full architectural extent:
     - a **sill** box runs from the slab top down to the bottom of the apron or panel under it (often 0.2-0.4 of the window height) and is wider than the window by about a quarter of the window width on each side;
     - a **cornice** over a window covers the whole crown, from the window top (consoles and frieze included) to the top of the hood, and is as wide as the sill;
     - a **molding** covers the whole band, every bead line included; the main cornice runs from the eaves down to below its brackets and frieze (often 1.5-2.5 times a string course); a projecting eave with a soffit band under a pitched roof is a molding too, a bare roof edge is not;
     - a balustrade panel under a window is a `balcony` (with `rail:"balustrade"`) even when it barely projects; a lowered roller blind is a `blind`;
     - ground-floor openings that reach the pavement are `door`s, or `shop`s when they carry a sign, a shop window or a grille: a shop box runs from the pavement to the top of its sign. Read their columns on their own, they often don't line up with the windows above.
   - Common reading errors and their fixes:
     - bands may be read too thin. Put the bottom edge at the lowest bead, dentil or shadow line of the band;
     - a balcony box runs from the railing top down to the underside of its slab; with `style:"classic"` the consoles under it are built automatically, in other styles add them as `deco` with `shape:"console"`; a door box runs to the threshold;
     - a window box is the opening/glass plus its own frame; open shutters beside it are separate `blind` boxes, never part of the window; a closed shutter covering a window gets both boxes, the `window` and the `blind` over it;
     - an arch's archivolt or a flat lintel flush with the wall is not a `cornice`: only a projecting hood or pediment is;
     - a continuous horizontal band is a `molding` even when it is decorated (meander, chequer or relief decoration); `deco` is for discrete ornaments, listed one by one (a grid when they repeat); read a band at every floor line where the photo shows one;
     - flat pilaster strips between windows, with or without capitals, are `pillar`s, not `deco`;
     - open ground-floor arcades are `arcade`; glazed or signed large arched storefronts are `shop`, not ordinary windows; a bay window is read sash by sash, like any other window;
     - small balustrade panels under single windows are `balcony`s even when they barely project (missed again when read as `deco`); hoods over dormers are `cornice`s;
     - small ornaments are easily missed: after the openings, sweep the facade once only for consoles, keystone heads, reliefs, cartouches and panels, and list each;
     - read neighbouring buildings only when they belong to the user's requested scope; otherwise exclude their boxes.
   - After reading, draw the boxes over the photo (`node "RUNTIME_ROOT/scripts/run-tool.mjs" overlay PHOTO SPEC.json OUT.jpg [--face right]`, on the image the boxes were read on; it takes a facade, a block, or a `detect_facade` draft) and fix what is visibly off before building.
   - Thin elements are a few pixels tall: when the photo is small, read them on a zoomed crop (`node "RUNTIME_ROOT/scripts/run-tool.mjs" crop PHOTO x0 y0 x1 y1 OUT.jpg` prints `origin` and `scale`: px = origin + crop pixel / scale).
   - Windows include their own frame, not the external decorative surround, in the opening box; glazing bars go in `bars`, not as separate windows. An opaque vent panel or spandrel inside the same frame is part of the window (one box, not a `blind`); a continuous recessed glass strip is one wide window with `bars`.
   - Brackets and consoles under a cornice, keystones, garlands and capitals are `deco`: list them (a grid of brackets is one element).
   - Signs, awnings and shop windows on the ground floor are one `shop` box per storefront, sign included.
   - Two or more sides visible (corner building, angled wings): one `block`, each visible side straightened and read as its own face; a side that isn't visible stays a plain wall (leave the face out, or `els:[]`).
   - Whole building from one face: the depth is not in the photo. Use the user's figure, a plan, a map or a side photo; otherwise a typical depth for the type, said as an assumption in the reply: row house 9-12 m, apartment or tenement block 12-14 m, historic palazzo 14-20 m, office slab 14-18 m, tower about as deep as it is wide. Check a block's faces on the photo with `overlay PHOTO SPEC.json OUT.jpg` (the front; `--face right` for another face read in px).
   - Historic facade: set `style:"classic"`, read the main cornice as one `molding` with `d` 0.6-1.2, the string courses between floors as thin `molding`s, the ground floor as `rustic` when it shows horizontal joints, and choose the roof the photo shows (`mansard` for steep tiled roofs with dormer windows, `hip` for four slopes).
   - Before building, check the spec once against the photo: each floor should have the same column count unless the photo clearly breaks the rhythm. For perspective photos or plans, use `building` and primitives from the main proportions.

## Build and review

Build with the three levels of runtime.md (`build_model`, else the same builder through `run_python`, else rhino-doctor), one call for the whole spec. Inspect warnings and clipped-element notes. Compare inventory against the result and use a viewport when the requested fidelity needs visual checking. See [acceptance criteria](acceptance.md).
